\begin{abstract}
The commoditization of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to a diverse marketplace of providers offering the same models under vastly different pricing structures---ranging from pay-per-token APIs to fixed-quota subscriptions and dedicated\jason{dedicated might not be the right world} \murphy{I would like to use reserve instead} concurrency slots. This heterogeneity creates an opportunity to minimize inference costs through intelligent routing.
However, naive strategies often fail to account for the opportunity cost of scarce subscription resources or the latency requirements of interactive applications.

In this paper, we formalize the problem of Latency--Cost Optimization for multi-provider LLM routing. We derive offline optimal algorithms for scenarios with quota and concurrency constraints, establishing theoretical lower bounds on cost. We then propose a learning-augmented online algorithm that leverages quantile regression to estimate request complexity and makes risk-aware routing decisions using a primal-dual framework. Empirical evaluation on real-world traces shows our approach achieves up to 30\% cost savings compared to standard baselines while maintaining strict latency SLOs, bridging the gap between theoretical optimality and practical deployment.
