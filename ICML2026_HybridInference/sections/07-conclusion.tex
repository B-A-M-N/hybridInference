\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a unified framework for optimizing LLM inference costs in a hybrid pricing environment that combines pay-per-token APIs with quota-limited and concurrency-limited subscriptions. Our framework applies broadly---from routing across third-party API providers to scheduling within enterprise GPU clusters with gutter pools for high availability.

\paragraph{Summary of Contributions.}
We formalized the hybrid routing problem in two stages: Stage 1 considers quota constraints alone (yielding an economic lower bound), while Stage 2 incorporates concurrency constraints (yielding the physical lower bound). For Stage 1, we proved that a simple greedy-by-value strategy is optimal. For Stage 2, we formulated a time-indexed ILP that jointly optimizes quota allocation and concurrent scheduling. For online routing, we proposed the Learning-Augmented Primal-Dual (LA-PD) algorithm that combines shadow pricing with conservative value estimation via quantile regression.

\paragraph{Key Findings.}
Our experiments on real-world traces revealed several insights: (1) Naive greedy routing leaves significant cost savings on the table---up to 4x worse than optimal on high-variance workloads. (2) Our online algorithm achieves a competitive ratio of 1.18x, approaching theoretical limits. (3) Predictor quality matters more than decision rule complexity; a simple EMA predictor outperforms more sophisticated histogram-based quantile estimation. (4) Under zero-wait semantics, quota constraints dominate concurrency constraints for current model sizes, but this balance shifts as models grow larger and effective concurrency decreases.

\paragraph{Practical Implications.}
For practitioners, our results suggest that even simple threshold-based routing policies can capture most of the potential savings, provided they account for opportunity cost. The exponential threshold function $\psi(z) = L(U/L)^z$ is easy to implement and requires only estimates of value bounds $[L, U]$ and current quota usage $z$.

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work.}
Our current formulation assumes requests are independent and ignores potential correlations (e.g., conversation sessions). Future work includes: (1) extending to hierarchical quotas (e.g., hourly + daily + weekly limits common in coding assistants), (2) handling soft latency SLOs with hedging strategies, (3) incorporating request preemption and migration in concurrency-limited settings, and (4) adapting to non-stationary workloads with online learning of value distributions.
